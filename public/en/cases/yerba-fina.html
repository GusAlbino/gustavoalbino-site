The challenge

Wear hemp without losing the street.

Yerba Fina was born from a simple, bold idea: truly sustainable Brazilian fashion, without the tame “eco” look. Hemp and bamboo-fibre clothing for people who skate, climb the mountain, ride the metro and own the city.

The challenge was an identity that could hold both worlds at once: nature and concrete. Organic enough to talk sustainability, urban enough to live on graffiti, the session and the street.

Yerba Fina garments use 65% hemp and 35% bamboo fibre. More ecological, more sustainable, and Brazilian.

Each piece becomes a wearable manifesto: “Wear hemp, wear sustainable clothes, wear your environment”.

The logo

A bud that adapts to anything.

At the heart of the brand is the bud, the flower of the Cannabis Sativa L. plant, hand-drawn. Unique, organic and direct, it’s a reference to hemp, the products’ main natural input.

The name follows the same brush stroke: handmade, slanted letters, natural and sophisticated at once. The brand uses only the darkest green of the palette.

Most importantly, the logo was built as a responsive system. It isn’t a single asset but a family of versions that adapt to the medium (from the website header to print, from the social avatar to the favicon), always keeping the same identity.

Logo versions

The logo versions

Each version was designed for a context of use: vertical, wide or symbol only. The guidelines define two official families. Version 1 has the bud and the name. Version 2, the full one, adds the slogan “Explore your medium”. Each exists in dark green, black and white, 18 files in all. With transparency, green and black can go down to 25% opacity. Minimum size is 10 mm².

Colour palette

From the woods to the sunset.

The official logo uses only the darkest green. The greens evoke nature, ecology and sustainability. Yellow is the sun, energy and lightness. Orange and red come from the sunset and bring a soft warmth, which also helps the logo’s usability. The logo can sit on any palette colour as long as contrast and legibility hold. The other system colours never go in with opacity.

Typography

Brush, slab and reading.

Three families, each with a fixed job: one for the logo, one for headlines and one for reading.

BrushNafta Light

Made by Krisjanis Mezulis of WildOnes Type Foundry, with brush and marker. Only for the logo and the slogan, never on other print or digital material.

Slab serifQuartzo

Free version, for headlines and highlights. It has no numerals.

ReadingRoboto

General text, headings and buttons, in six weights, from Thin to Black. It ships on most sites and design software.

The brand on the street.

The identity comes alive across social media campaigns, product mockups and comms pieces, always with the tension between the organic and the urban.
