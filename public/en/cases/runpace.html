Branding · design system

Runpace

A sports eyewear brand that already existed and needed a system. The rebrand was done by removing, not adding.

RoleDesign and strategy consultant

The governing principle

Runpace removes. The competitor adds.

Every decision in the system answers to that line. Faced with any doubt, one more colour, one more weight, one more effect, one more line of copy, the default answer is no.

In a market where competitors stack gradients, shadows and promises, subtracting is what sets you apart. And subtracting only holds when it is written down, because otherwise the next supplier adds it back.

What was removed

"FOR ATHLETES" was removed from the logotype for good. "SEE FASTER, GO FARTHER" was removed from the site. Two English promises on a Brazilian brand selling to Brazilian runners, and neither said anything the product did not show on its own.

Five words went on the banned list: premium, exclusive, revolutionary, game changer and unmissable. They are the words every sports brand uses, which is exactly why they differentiate none of them.

The logotype

A single wordmark, uppercase, obliqued 17°, with staggered cuts crossing the letters at -6.5° and speed marks on all four sides. No attached symbol and no descriptor.

Monochrome in every version. Blue on light, white on dark and over photography, graphite for one-colour print and embroidery, orange only on action pieces. Four versions, and no others.

The palette carries a mandatory ratio

Listing colours is not enough, because a colour list does not stop anyone from making a piece that is 60% orange. The system declares how much of any surface each colour may occupy.

Contrast for every pair was calculated with the WCAG 2.1 formula and written into the manual. Gray 400 is marked decorative only, never text, because 2.54:1 fails.

Product naming became a system

Each model is now named after a World Marathon Majors city: Tokyo, Berlin, Chicago, London. The line grows without inventing names, and the customer reads the hierarchy without explanation.

The manual requires checking each name with the Brazilian trademark office before production. A good name that cannot be registered turns into wasted packaging.

The brand in use

The gap, written down

The official artwork exists today only as a 2091 by 752 pixel PNG. It covers screen and print up to roughly 17cm at 300dpi, and nothing above that.

The manual carries the table of which processes require vector: packaging, large banner, sticker, embroidery, vinyl cutting, hot stamping and screen printing above 17cm. Documenting the gap serves the client better than shipping a manual that pretends it is not there.
