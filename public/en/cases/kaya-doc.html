Kaya Doc

Prescribing from evidence

Medical-cannabis platform for prescribers. Catalogue, product comparator, library of scientific studies and a prescription co-pilot, in a field where information is scattered and regulation is strict. I have led product design since 2022.

Version 1

The start: the platform existed, the door did not.

The first version shipped the essentials: product comparator, library of scientific studies and product discovery. The institutional green, the typography and the menu structure came straight from the Kaya brand.

And prescriber sign-up happened outside the product, over a WhatsApp conversation. That handles the first hundred users; it does not scale to a thousand.

Version 2

What changed, and why.

V2 rebuilds on a system. What used to be screens drawn one by one became a library: 353 master components, 322 of them declared variants, tokens in two layers, and every artefact existing on desktop, tablet and mobile.

The door moves inside the product: sign-up phased across four steps, with labelled progress. And the public site swaps illustration for photography, taking an evidence-led message instead of an institutional one.

The product

Clinical decisions need organised information.

A medical-cannabis prescriber decides on hard ground: a catalogue scattered across manufacturers, regulation in motion, and scientific evidence spread over databases that do not talk to each other. The platform exists to bring that into one place.

The figures Kaya Doc publishes give the scale: over 1,500 catalogued products, 200 integrated brands, 250 scientific studies and 100 mapped medical conditions.

The system

A design system built to become code.

The library holds 353 master components, 322 of them declared variants, not loose copies. That is what separates a folder of screens from a system: when the button changes, it changes everywhere.

Tokens are built in two layers. On top, the product semantic palette. Underneath, a primitive base named along Tailwind lines: rounded-lg, shadow-md, the 4px scale. That is what makes handoff work. The developer reads the token name and knows what to build, with no translation in between.

Hierarchy

The file name already answers the question.

Every artefact exists on desktop, tablet and mobile as a designed piece, not an automatic adaptation. The sign-up progress bar, for one, has its own version per width.

And the naming carries the information: “Deskt_Pg00_Ex — KD 2.0 — Cadastro passo 1 de 2” states device, page, scope, product and flow step. Anyone on the team finds the file without asking. In a growing team, that beats any process document.

The flow

Sign-up in four steps, with the end in sight.

Prescriber sign-up is phased across four steps, with a progress bar labelled 25% to 100%. A long form with no sense of progress is an abandoned form; telling people where they are cuts drop-off.

The form system was designed for the unhappy path too: each field carries a hidden caption slot reserved for the error message, and there is a specific pattern for the "other" option, the one where the user must write something the list did not anticipate.

Where the care shows

Three decisions nobody notices, and that change everything.

Evidence iconography. Clinical trial, cohort study and systematic review each have their own icon. Not decoration: the prescriber recognises the weight of the source before reading the title.

Versioned consent. The cookie dialog carries the policy version the user accepted. Data-protection law enters as a product decision, at the moment consent happens.

Progress as reward. Medals, levels and a progress bar applied to medical education. The professional tracks their own advance through the study library.

My role

What I lead.

I drive product design for Kaya Doc end to end: interface, experience, user research, building and maintaining the design system, advanced Figma prototyping, and bringing AI tools into the team workflow itself.

I work directly with stakeholders, technology and engineering, which is where a design system stops being a pretty library and becomes infrastructure.
